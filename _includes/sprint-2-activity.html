Our two methods

Our two methods: 1. Function Points 2. Use-Case Points

Why these two use different starting information: Function Points estimate the system based on what users enter, view, and what data the system stores, while Use-Case Points estimate the system based on the actors who interact with it and the use cases they perform.

Gap 560 ÷ 405 = 1.38

Do your two methods depend on the same assumption?

No. Function Points depend on the system's inputs, outputs, inquiries, stored data, and productivity rate, while Use-Case Points depend on the actors, use-case complexity, and hours per UCP.

Which single input moves the answer most?

The Function Point productivity rate moves the answer the most. At 6 hours per FP, the estimate is 270 hours, while at 12 hours per FP, it increases to 540 hours.

What information would narrow the gap, and when could you get it?

Actual team productivity data would narrow the gap. Once development begins, we could measure the actual hours needed to complete features and use cases instead of relying on assumed productivity rates.

Our explanation of the gap

The Function Point method produced an estimate of 270 to 540 hours with a middle value of 405 hours, while the Use-Case Point method produced an estimate of 560 hours. The gap between the middle values is about 1.38. The estimates differ because Function Points measure inputs, outputs, inquiries, and stored data, while Use-Case Points measure actors and the complexity of their interactions with the system. Both methods also rely on assumed productivity rates because we do not yet have actual development data. Collecting real productivity data during development would help reduce this uncertainty.

Cost and calendar calculations

Cost: 270 × $60 = $16,200; 560 × $60 = $33,600. The $60/hour loaded rate is an assumption.

Team capacity: 4 people × 30 productive hours/person/week = 120 team hours/week. Low: 270 ÷ 120 = 2.25 weeks, rounded to 2.3. High: 560 ÷ 120 = 4.67 weeks, rounded to 4.7. Expected: 438.3 ÷ 120 = 3.65 weeks, rounded to 3.7.
