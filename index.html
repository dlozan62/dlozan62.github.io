Sprint 1 • Market Research

Market Research

Aggregate Findings

Across our interviews, the strongest pattern was that students want a meal plan that feels worth the money. Students have different budgets and eating habits, but several concerns appeared repeatedly: unused money should not expire, students want flexibility in how they spend their balance, and food variety matters.

We also found that one plan may not work for everyone. Some students preferred a dollar balance because their spending changes from day to day, while others preferred a set number of meals because it is easier to track.

Top Pick: Flexible Meal Plan Structure

Our strongest idea is a flexible campus meal plan that supports different types of students. The concept may include multiple plan levels, rollover of unused value, and both monthly and upfront payment options.

We selected this direction because it was supported by repeated student feedback rather than a single interview response.

What We Pivoted From

We moved away from treating one fixed meal plan as the best solution for every student. Interviews showed that commuters, students living on campus, and graduate students may have very different dining needs.

Because of this feedback, the project now focuses on flexibility and different levels of meal-plan usage.

Sprint 1 • Business Strategy

Business Strategy

Strategy-to-Project Chain

Students are more likely to see value in a campus meal plan when it fits their budget, schedule, and eating habits. Our research suggests that flexibility, rollover, multiple dining options, and easier payment choices are important parts of that value.

The project exists to test whether a flexible meal-plan structure could make campus dining more attractive to a wider range of students. If validated, the concept could potentially increase meal-plan adoption and keep more student dining spending on campus.

Sprint 1 • Project Charter

Project Charter

The project charter defines the purpose, boundaries, assumptions, stakeholders, and measures of success for the initiative.

1. Project Title

Flexible Campus Dining Meal Plan Initiative

2. Business Objective

The main goal of our project is to determine whether students would be more interested in purchasing a campus meal plan if it offered greater flexibility and better value for their money.

Our interviews showed that students have different budgets and eating habits. Because of this, we want to create and validate a meal-plan concept that can work for students who eat on campus frequently as well as students who only eat on campus occasionally.

3. Scope Boundary

In Scope

  • Evaluate dollar-balance and set-meal plan structures.
  • Explore plan levels for different student dining habits.
  • Explore rollover options for unused money or meals.
  • Compare monthly and semester-based payment options.
  • Consider multiple campus dining locations.
  • Consider food variety, healthy choices, and dietary information.
  • Gather additional student feedback before implementation recommendations.

Out of Scope

  • Building or launching a complete meal-plan system.
  • Changing tuition or unrelated university fees.
  • Renovating campus dining facilities.
  • Changing university financial policies.
  • Guaranteeing participation from specific restaurants.
  • Building a production mobile application or website during this phase.

Deferred

  • Choosing exact prices for every meal-plan option.
  • Connecting the plan to university payment or ID systems.
  • Adding off-campus restaurants.
  • Building a production application or web portal.
  • Campus-wide implementation.

4. Assumptions

  • The university could offer more than one meal-plan structure.
  • Current systems could support a balance, meal count, or hybrid plan.
  • Rollover may be possible between semesters.
  • Monthly payment options could be supported.
  • Other students may share concerns similar to those we interviewed.
  • A flexible plan could be priced to work for both students and campus dining.

5. Stakeholder Register

Stakeholder Why They Are Involved Influence
Students Main users of the meal plan and primary source of feedback. High
Commuter Students Need plans that avoid wasting money when they are not on campus every day. High
Students Living on Campus May need larger or more frequent-use meal plans. High
Graduate Students May prefer flexible spending because of different schedules. Medium
Campus Dining Administration Would evaluate whether the plan is financially and operationally feasible. High
University Administration Would approve major policy, payment, or implementation changes. High
Dining Locations / Vendors Would be affected by where and how meal-plan value can be used. Medium
Project Team Responsible for research, planning, validation, and documentation. High

6. Success Criteria

  • At least 80% of students in the next validation round support rollover.
  • At least 70% say the proposed plan provides better value or flexibility than one fixed plan.
  • Identify at least two meal-plan approaches for different student dining habits.
  • Clearly explain payment options and what happens to unused value.
  • Clearly explain where meal-plan funds or meals can be used.
  • Support every major part of the final concept with research or validation.
