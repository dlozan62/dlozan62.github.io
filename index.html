Sprint 1 • Market Research

Market Research

Aggregate Findings

Across our interviews, the biggest pattern was that students want a meal plan that feels worth the money. Students had different budgets and different eating habits, but several concerns showed up repeatedly: unused money should not expire, students want flexibility in how they spend their balance, and food variety matters.

We also found that one plan may not work for everyone. Some students preferred a dollar balance because their spending changes from day to day, while others preferred a set number of meals because it is easier to keep track of.

Top Pick: Flexible Meal Plan Structure

Our strongest idea is a flexible campus meal plan that can support different types of students. The concept may include more than one plan level, rollover of unused value, and both monthly and upfront payment options. We chose this direction because it was supported by repeated interview feedback instead of just one person's opinion.

What We Pivoted From

We moved away from treating a single fixed meal plan as the best solution for every student. The interviews showed that commuters, students living on campus, and graduate students may have very different needs. Because of this, our project now focuses more on flexibility and different usage levels.

Sprint 1 • Business Strategy

Business Strategy

Strategy-to-Project Chain

Students are more likely to see value in a campus meal plan when it fits their budget, schedule, and eating habits. Our research suggests that flexibility, rollover, multiple dining options, and easier payment choices are important parts of that value.

The project exists to test whether a flexible meal-plan structure could make campus dining more attractive to a wider range of students. If the idea is validated, it could potentially increase meal-plan adoption and keep more student dining spending on campus.

Sprint 1 • Project Charter

Project Charter

This charter defines the direction and boundaries of the project.

1. Project Title

Flexible Campus Dining Meal Plan Initiative

2. Business Objective

The main goal of our project is to find out if students would be more interested in buying a campus meal plan if it was more flexible and gave them better value for their money.

Our interviews showed that students have different budgets and eating habits. Because of this, we want to create and validate a meal-plan idea that can work for students who eat on campus often and students who only eat on campus sometimes.

3. Scope Boundary

In Scope

  • Looking at dollar-balance and set-meal plan structures.
  • Exploring different plan levels for different student dining habits.
  • Looking at rollover for unused meal-plan money or meals.
  • Comparing monthly payments with paying for the semester all at once.
  • Considering multiple campus dining locations.
  • Considering food variety, healthy choices, and dietary information.
  • Getting additional student feedback before recommending implementation.

Out of Scope

  • Building or launching a complete meal-plan system.
  • Changing tuition or unrelated university fees.
  • Renovating campus dining facilities.
  • Changing university financial policies.
  • Guaranteeing that certain restaurants will participate.
  • Building a full production mobile app or website during this phase.

Deferred

  • Choosing exact prices for every meal-plan option.
  • Connecting the plan to existing university payment or ID systems.
  • Adding off-campus restaurants.
  • Building a production app or web portal.
  • Campus-wide implementation.

4. Assumptions

  • We assume the university could offer more than one meal-plan structure.
  • We assume current systems could support a balance, meal count, or hybrid plan.
  • We assume rollover may be possible between semesters.
  • We assume monthly payments could be supported.
  • We assume other students may have concerns similar to the students we interviewed.
  • We assume a flexible plan could be priced at a level that works for both students and campus dining.

6. Success Criteria

  • At least 80% of students in our next validation round support the rollover idea.
  • At least 70% say the proposed plan gives better value or flexibility than one fixed plan.
  • We identify at least two meal-plan approaches for different student dining habits.
  • The final concept clearly explains payment options and what happens to unused value.
  • The final concept explains where meal-plan funds or meals can be used.
  • Every major part of the concept is supported by research or later validation.
