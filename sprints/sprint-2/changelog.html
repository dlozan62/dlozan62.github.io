Sprint 2 · Changelog

Project changes and decisions.

Changelog · PDF Back to Sprint 2

  1. October 4 and 5, 2026

    Built the Sprint 2 page and this changelog

    What changed
    The Sprint 2 page now holds the one-page budget, with a PDF download. This changelog is its own page, and the home page leads to Sprint 2.
    Why
    Sprint 2 requires a one-page budget and a changelog, each as web text and PDF.
    What it affected
    Sprint 2 page, home page, sprint archive, and the site checker’s list of required pages.

  2. October 3, 4 & 5, 2026

    Corrected the one-page budget

    What changed
    The budget now uses the revised 694-hour estimate and the $60/hour rate instead of 427 hours and $13,000 per FTE-month. We fixed the FTE arithmetic (427 ÷ 130 is 3.28, not 3.29, and the Testing row did not match between the two tables), then added named risks, reserves, and operating cost, and fit it on one page.
    Why
    The 427 hours did not match any figure in the estimation worksheet, and $13,000 per FTE-month was the example rate from the Session 11 slides.
    What it affected
    One-page budget and its PDF, and the budget and business case sections of the Sprint 2 page.

  3. October 3, 4 & 5, 2026

    Recounted the estimate and completed the appendix

    What changed
    We added the dining-staff actor, the staff update form, a “mark an item sold out” use case, and an “update hours and menu” use case. Function points went from 45 to 49 and use-case points from 28 to 46. The range moved from 270–560 hours (expected 438) to 294–1,150 hours (expected 694), and cost from $16,200–$33,600 to $17,640–$69,000. Every counted item and use case is now named, use-case points have a 15–25 hours per UCP range, and the worksheet explains M, the integration hours, and the N/A on the larger-team check.
    Why
    The September 28 count listed only students as actors. Students can’t mark an item sold out, so someone else has to, and the screen they use is something we would build. The totals also needed named items so someone outside the team could check them.
    What it affected
    Estimation worksheet and its PDF, and the estimation appendix, business case, and one-page budget sections of the Sprint 2 page.

  4. October 4, 2026

    Recorded the go decision and its conditions

    What changed
    The business case now states the team’s decision: go ahead with the slice and the project, on the condition that we stress test it and work closely with the university and the restaurant on campus, because success depends on staff keeping menu inventory and times up to date. It also notes the team’s idea of passing the costs to the university as a service instead of to students.
    Why
    The team needed its go/no-go recommendation and the conditions on it written down.
    What it affected
    Business case section of the Sprint 2 page.

  5. October 4, 2026

    Reviewed the estimate and agreed on changes

    What changed
    Reviewing the September 28 estimate, we decided to add the dining-staff actor and the staff update form to the slice, name every counted item, and use the instructor-provided $60/hour rate in every cost figure.
    Why
    Sold-out status only exists if someone enters it, and the original count had only the student as an actor. One loaded rate keeps the estimate and budget consistent.
    What it affected
    Estimation worksheet, one-page budget, and business case. The changes were made on October 5.

  6. October 3, 2026

    Renamed the site and documents DineCheck

    What changed
    The site and the Sprint 2 documents now use the name DineCheck, with the tagline “What’s open, what’s left.” “Campus Dining Availability System” stays as the descriptor under the name.
    Why
    This applies the name the team chose on October 3.
    What it affected
    Site title, header, footer, home page, About page description, the repository README, and the project line in the Sprint 2 documents. The Sprint 1 market research, business strategy, charter, and their PDFs keep the old name on purpose: they are the Sprint 1 record, and this entry explains the difference.

  7. October 3, 2026

    Chose the name DineCheck

    What changed
    The team chose DineCheck as the project name, with the tagline “What’s open, what’s left.”
    Why
    “Campus Dining Availability System” is long to say and hard to remember. DineCheck is short and says what a student does with it.
    What it affected
    The name was applied across the site and the Sprint 2 documents on October 5.

  8. October 2, 2026

    Why

    What it affected
    Sprint 1 page only. The research, strategy, and charter content did not change.
