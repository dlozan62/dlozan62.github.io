01 · Reliability

Service level objectives

Reliability expectations proposed in our Service Levels document.

Our team documented two proposed service level objectives (SLOs) for the project portal. Both use a rolling 14-day measurement window.

SLO 1 — Portal availability 99.5% target

At least 99.5% of scheduled one-minute checks should succeed. A successful check requires the homepage and all published sprint pages to return HTTP 200.

Proposed measurement owner: Zachary Carrejo.

SLO 2 — Document downloads 99% target

At least 99% of daily checks of published document links should succeed. Success requires HTTP 200 and a nonempty file matching the advertised format.

Proposed measurement owner: Jacob Ordonez.

Measurement planning

The plan calls for scheduled checks with recorded timestamps and results. Monitoring has not been confirmed as operational, so the team is not claiming SLO compliance.

Incident severity and response

We documented three proposed severity levels for problems affecting the project portal.

Severity 1 — Critical 1 support hour

Example: The entire portal is unavailable.

Proposed response: Begin investigating and fixing within one support hour.

Severity 2 — Major 4 support hours

Example: A sprint page or required PDF download is inaccessible.

Proposed response: Begin investigating within four support hours.

Severity 3 — Minor 1 school day

Example: A typo or visual problem that does not prevent access.

Proposed response: Begin addressing the issue within one school day.

Proposed team responsibilities

Zachary Carrejo — Incident commander: Classifies incidents and assigns work.

Jacob Ordonez — Operations lead: Investigates website issues and verifies repairs.

Daniel Lozano — Backup fixer: Tracks follow-up work and assists when assigned.

Salvador Rodarte — Communicator: Coordinates incident and recovery updates.

These responsibilities and response expectations are proposed procedures described in the submitted Service Levels document.

02 · Reliability planning

Error budgets

Calculating acceptable failures over a two-week period.

Our error budget work focused on how much failure could be allowed while still meeting the proposed SLOs.

Portal availability budget 14 days

A 14-day period contains 20,160 minutes. At 99.5% availability, the allowed failure budget is 0.5%, or 100.8 minutes.

With whole one-minute checks, no more than 100 failures are allowed while meeting the target.

Document download budget 1% allowance

The allowed failures equal the floor of the total document checks multiplied by 0.01.

For example, 15 documents checked daily for 14 days produce 210 checks and allow 2 failures. This is an example, not an actual measurement.

Proposed error budget policy

At 50% budget consumption, the team would prioritize reliability work. If a budget is exhausted, feature releases would pause until the affected service is repaired and the target is met again.

Connection to software delivery

The Service Levels plan connects error budgets with deployment frequency, lead time, and change failure rate. It proposes reviewed pull requests and validation for future releases when a budget is exhausted.

These are proposed policies and example calculations, not measured performance or implemented release controls.

04 · Design

Interface sketch

An early mobile interface concept for DineCheck.

Salvador Rodarte prepared a hand-drawn sketch showing how students could navigate a possible DineCheck mobile interface.

Ideas shown in the sketch

Campus selection: A screen for choosing a campus.

Dining locations: A list of dining options and whether locations are open or closed.

Menu information: Food items, ingredients, allergens, calories, and availability.

Navigation: Searching locations, browsing menus, and viewing a map.

This is a design concept, not a completed application. The sketch is available in the documents section below.

05 · Reflection

Sprint 4 retrospective

Reviewing teamwork, progress, and improvements for Sprint 5.

Our team retrospective covered how responsibilities were divided, the communication challenges we experienced, and what we plan to improve.

What went well Teamwork

Responsibilities were divided more clearly, allowing each member to focus on assigned tasks.

What could have gone better Communication

Team updates were not always communicated early enough, which affected coordination and timing.

Improvement for Sprint 5 Next steps

We plan to set clearer internal deadlines, communicate progress more frequently, and ask for help earlier when needed.

This summary reflects the team's submitted Sprint 4 retrospective.
