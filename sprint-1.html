Market research

What students told us

Patterns from the interviews

Students often said that a meal plan needs to feel worth the money. Their budgets and eating habits were different, but the same concerns came up in several interviews. Students did not want unused money to expire, wanted flexibility in how they spent their balance, and cared about having a variety of food choices.

We also found that one plan may not work for everyone. Some students preferred a dollar balance because their spending changes from day to day, while others preferred a set number of meals because it is easier to keep track of.

Research protocol

How we planned the interviews

Questions about past experiences

Our interview packet asks participants about recent campus dining experiences. We ask how they checked a menu, when food was unavailable, which locations they trusted, and what information changed their dining decision.

Facilitation rules

We do not pitch a solution during the interview. We ask about past behavior, specific experiences, repeated problems, workarounds, and consequences. We also record examples that do not support our idea.

Participant care

Participants are described without full names. The interviewer asks for verbal permission and does not record a conversation without consent.

Top pick

The flexible meal-plan idea

Our team selected a flexible campus meal plan that could work for students with different budgets and eating habits. The idea may include more than one plan level, rollover for unused value, and monthly or upfront payment options. We chose it because the same needs appeared in several interviews.

What we changed after the interviews

We stopped treating one fixed meal plan as the best option for every student. Our interviews showed that commuters, students living on campus, and graduate students may have different needs. We decided to focus on flexible plans for different levels of use.

Business strategy

How the research supports the project

Students may see more value in a campus meal plan when it fits their budget, schedule, and eating habits. Our interviews showed that flexibility, rollover, multiple dining options, and payment choices all affect how students judge that value.

Our team wants to test whether a flexible meal-plan structure would interest more students. If later research supports the idea, it may increase meal-plan participation and keep more student dining spending on campus.

Project charter

Project direction and scope

Project title

Flexible Campus Dining Meal Plan Initiative

Business objective

The main goal of our project is to find out if students would be more interested in buying a campus meal plan if it was more flexible and gave them better value for their money.

Our interviews showed that students have different budgets and eating habits. Because of this, we want to create and validate a meal-plan idea that can work for students who eat on campus often and students who only eat on campus sometimes.

Scope boundary

What this project covers

In scope

  • Dollar-balance and set-meal structures
  • Plan levels for different dining habits
  • Rollover for unused value or meals
  • Monthly and semester payment options
  • Multiple campus dining locations
  • Food variety, healthy choices, and dietary information
  • Additional student feedback before implementation

Out of scope

  • Launching a complete meal-plan system
  • Changing tuition or unrelated fees
  • Renovating campus dining facilities
  • Changing university financial policies
  • Guaranteeing restaurant participation
  • Building a production app or website in this phase

Deferred

  • Exact pricing for each option
  • University ID and payment integrations
  • Off-campus restaurants
  • A production app or web portal
  • Campus-wide implementation

Assumptions

Assumptions we still need to test

  • The university could offer more than one meal-plan structure.
  • Current systems could support a balance, meal count, or hybrid plan.
  • Rollover may be possible between semesters.
  • Monthly payments could be supported.
  • Other students may share the concerns heard in interviews.
  • A flexible plan could work financially for students and campus dining.

Success criteria

How we will measure the idea

  1. 80% of students in the next validation round support rollover.
  2. 70% say the proposal offers better value or flexibility than one fixed plan.
  3. 2+ meal-plan approaches address different dining habits.
  4. Clear payment choices, unused-value rules, and eligible locations.
  5. Grounded major decisions are supported by research or later validation.
